Reinforcement learning can improve pretrained robot policies, but imperfect critic guidance can also disrupt the useful behaviors they have already learned. 
Even with a frozen base policy, repeated critic-guided corrections can gradually override useful pretrained behavior, making small updates alone insufficient to ensure its preservation. 
We present \method{} (Constrained Action-Space Transport), a likelihood-free method that learns residual corrections to a frozen generative policy. 
\method{} constructs action targets by combining critic guidance with a soft pull toward the corresponding base actions and capping each proposed correction. 
The residual then learns these targets by regression. 
This design maintains a persistent reference to pretrained behavior while leaving room for adaptation. 
Experiments in simulated manipulation show improved task success and fewer environment steps needed to reach a target success rate. 
Real-robot experiments on OpenArm further show improved placement and stacking success over the pretrained policies. 
Ablations show that restoration can preserve task success where clipping alone fails, supporting the importance of a persistent reference during refinement. 
Broader evaluations reveal that the benefits of anchoring vary across tasks, highlighting the need to balance preserving useful behavior with allowing the changes required for improvement. \url{https://cast2027.github.io}
