We presented CAST, a likelihood-free residual method that refines frozen generative policies using action targets that combine critic guidance, soft restoration, and displacement clipping. Across simulated manipulation tasks, CAST improves task success and interaction efficiency, while real-robot evaluations show gains in placement and stacking. Ablations further show that restoration preserves useful pretrained behavior where clipping alone fails. Together, these results highlight the central tradeoff: a persistent reference should resist unreliable drift without preventing necessary corrections. Future work will adapt restoration to critic reliability and conduct larger-scale physical evaluations.
