\method{} retains DICE-RL's off-policy residual framework~\cite{dicerl2026}: the pretrained policy $\pi_0(s,z)$ is frozen, while the residual actor $r_\theta(s,z)$ and critic ensemble $Q_\phi(s,z,a)$ are trained. Here, $s$ is the observation, $z$ the noise, and $a$ the action chunk; noise-independent critics omit $z$. From replayed demonstrations and policy rollouts, the critic learns expected discounted returns using temporal-difference targets that combine rewards with predictions from a slowly updated target critic. Our main variant replaces DICE-RL's actor objective with regression to targets built from critic action gradients, a soft pull toward the same-noise base action, and clipping of the combined displacement. Targets remain fixed during each actor update. Action-space operations use normalized coordinates ($D=Hd$), and the frozen base supplies samples without requiring policy likelihoods or gradients through its parameters. Fig.~\ref{fig:method-update} illustrates the four-step actor update: sample, propose, constrain, and fit.

\subsection{Sample Actions and Propose Corrections}
\label{sec:reward-field}


\textbf{Step 1: sample paired base and current actions.}
For each observation $s$ from replayed demonstrations or policy rollouts, draw $K$ independent noise samples $z_i\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$. Using the frozen pretrained policy $\pi_0$, generate the base action $a_{0,i}=\pi_0(s,z_i)$ and a fresh candidate:
\begin{equation}
  \bar r_i=\operatorname{sg}[r_\theta(s,z_i)],
  \qquad a_i=a_{0,i}+\bar r_i,
  \label{eq:candidates}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}$ stops gradients during target construction. Each paired base action provides a fixed restoration reference for the same $(s,z_i)$.

\textbf{Step 2: ask the critic for a local correction.}
Let $\bar Q_\phi(s,z,a)$ denote the conservative critic-ensemble aggregate; we omit $z$ for noise-independent critics. We divide its action gradient by the detached scale $q_{\mathrm{scale}}=\max(\epsilon,\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{batch}}[|\bar Q_\phi|])$, computed over the current candidates, where $\epsilon>0$ prevents division by zero. This reduces sensitivity to the overall magnitude of predicted values. Holding critic parameters fixed, the proposed critic displacement is
\begin{equation}
  d_i^Q=\eta_Q C_{\delta_Q}\!\left(
  \frac{\nabla_a\bar Q_\phi(s,z_i,a_i)}{q_{\mathrm{scale}}}
  \right),
  \label{eq:reward-step}
\end{equation}
where $C_\delta(v)=v\min(1,\delta/\|v\|_2)$ and $C_\delta(0)=0$. The scale $\eta_Q$ sets the strength of critic guidance. The field cap $\delta_Q$ clips the normalized gradient \emph{before} applying $\eta_Q$, so $\|d_i^Q\|_2\leq\eta_Q\delta_Q$. The critic thus proposes a local change aimed at increasing predicted return. The next step balances this proposal against departure from the base policy.

\subsection{Restore Toward the Base and Limit the Step}
\label{sec:anchor}



\textbf{Step 3: combine improvement with a persistent reference.}
The detached residual $\bar r_i=a_i-a_{0,i}$ measures the current candidate's displacement from its paired base action. We express this departure as the root mean square (RMS) magnitude $m_i=\|\bar r_i\|_2/\sqrt{D}$ and define the restoration displacement as
\begin{equation}
d_i^R=-\eta_0\bar r_i
-\eta_{\mathrm{anc}}
\left(1-\frac{\rho}{\max(m_i,\epsilon)}\right)_{+}\bar r_i,
\label{eq:restoring-step}
\end{equation}
where $(x)_+=\max(x,0)$. The coefficient $\eta_0$ controls a weak pull toward the base at every nonzero residual, while $\eta_{\mathrm{anc}}$ controls an additional pull beyond the RMS radius $\rho$. This radius leaves room for adaptation under the weak pull before stronger restoration activates. It is a soft threshold, so targets may still lie outside it. Using RMS units makes $\rho$ comparable across action-chunk dimensions.

We combine both displacements at the current candidate: critic guidance favors higher predicted return, and restoration resists accumulated departure from the base. We then clip their sum to construct the residual target:
\begin{equation}
\tilde r_i=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[
\bar r_i+C_{\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}}(d_i^Q+d_i^R)
\right].
\label{eq:field-target}
\end{equation}
The cap $\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}$ sets the maximum requested change, ensuring $\|\tilde r_i-\bar r_i\|_2\leq\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}$ even when restoration is large. The target is held fixed during the actor update. Together, restoration and clipping balance adaptation with a persistent reference by acting on accumulated departure and individual corrections, respectively. The component study in Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-controls} tests this distinction.


\subsection{Learn from Fixed Targets}
\label{sec:field-update}
\textbf{Step 4: fit the residual to the corrected actions.}
Since the base is frozen, fitting the corrected action $\tilde a_i=a_{0,i}+\tilde r_i$ is equivalent to regressing the residual:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{actor}}
=\mathbb{E}_{s}\!\left[
\frac{1}{KD}\sum_{i=1}^{K}
\|r_\theta(s,z_i)-\tilde r_i\|_2^2
\right],
\label{eq:actor-regression}
\end{equation}
where $K$ is the number of sampled candidates per observation and $D=Hd$ is the action-chunk dimension, comprising $H$ controls of $d$ dimensions each. This mean-squared error is the actor's only training loss. Targets are held fixed during each update and recomputed for the next, so gradients do not pass through target construction.

\textbf{Training and execution.}
Algorithm~\ref{alg:cast} summarizes online training, retaining the baseline's replay mixture, warm-up, exploration, and multi-sample $n$-step TD critic updates. Only the critic uses a slowly updated target network. Physical refinement (Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-real-robot}) trains offline on saved experience, with residual corrections limited to the portion of each chunk designated for execution. At inference, we sample candidate actions as $\pi_0(s,z)+r_\theta(s,z)$ using the base policy's sampling procedure and apply each benchmark's selection rule, including value-guided selection in the matched LIBERO comparisons.
