A failed robot task can contain useful behavior that refinement should
retain. With sparse rewards, however, the outcome does not identify which
actions need to change, and imperfect critic guidance can disrupt an
existing skill. We present \method{} (Constrained Action-Space Transport),
a likelihood-free method for refining a frozen generative policy through
learned residual corrections. CAST constructs explicit action targets by
combining critic guidance with soft restoration toward the base and
clipping the resulting displacement. The residual then learns these targets
by regression. Restoration opposes accumulated departure from the base,
while clipping limits each requested correction. Per-task refinement on
eight LIBERO-90 tasks improves mean success by 9.4 percentage points over
the drifting-model base, compared with 0.9 for DICE-RL. On a shared diffusion
base for robomimic square, CAST reaches 90\% success with approximately
38\% fewer environment steps than DICE-RL. In a five-task LIBERO ablation,
removing all restoration yields zero success even with clipping. Initial
OpenArm evaluations improve placement and stacking success by 40 and
35 percentage points over their bases. Task-dependent reversals under
alternative constraints and broader evaluations show the limits of
anchoring. The results support a persistent behavioral reference that
leaves room for the corrections needed to improve the skill.
