Improving a pretrained robot policy requires balancing prior competence
with new experience. The base policy provides useful but imperfect behavior;
a learned critic suggests improvements but can be exploited by optimization.
Relying too strongly on either can limit refinement or destroy existing
skills. We present \method{} (Constrained Action-Space Transport), a
likelihood-free method for adapting generative robot policies with
task-success rewards. \method{} uses critic-guided action targets to train
a residual over a frozen base. A soft restoring field permits local
adaptation while increasingly resisting larger departures, and clipping
limits each target correction. In a five-task LIBERO ablation, removing
restoration causes complete collapse despite step clipping, showing that
small updates alone do not preserve competence. Yet anchoring need not
prevent improvement: per-task \method{} fine-tuning gains $9.4$ percentage
points over the base on eight challenging LIBERO-90 tasks, outperforming the standard DICE-RL
actor update, and over $50$ points on robomimic square. Initial OpenArm
evaluations show gains of $35$--$40$ points across placement and stacking.
These results support anchoring adaptation to prior competence as a
practical principle for critic-guided policy refinement.
