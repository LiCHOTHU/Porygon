Reinforcement learning can improve robot policies learned from demonstrations,
but errors in a learned critic can also destroy their existing skills.
We present \method{} (Constrained Action-Space Transport), a method for
fine-tuning generative robot policies using task-success rewards. \method{}
learns corrections to a frozen pretrained policy: it adjusts sampled actions
using the critic and trains a small network to reproduce them. A restoring
term permits small corrections while resisting larger departures from the
pretrained behavior. On eight challenging LIBERO-90 tasks, per-task
fine-tuning raises mean success from $66.1\%$ to $75.5\%$, compared with
$67.0\%$ for the standard residual actor update. On robomimic square,
success rises from $38.2\%$ to $91.2$--$93.0\%$. Removing the restoring term
causes zero success on all five LIBERO ablation tasks, even with step clipping.
The key is to limit accumulated departure from the pretrained policy,
not merely the size of each update.
