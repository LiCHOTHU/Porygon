Robot policy refinement should correct failures while retaining behavior
that already works. Sparse task rewards make this difficult: a failed
attempt does not identify which actions should change, and imperfect critic
guidance can disrupt an otherwise useful skill. We present \method{}
(Constrained Action-Space Transport), a likelihood-free method for learning
residual corrections to a frozen generative policy. \method{} constructs
action targets using critic guidance, soft restoration toward the base,
and displacement clipping, then fits the residual by regression.
Restoration opposes accumulated departure from pretrained behavior;
clipping limits each requested correction. Across eight LIBERO-90 tasks,
per-task refinement improves mean success by 9.4 percentage points over
the drifting-model base, compared with 0.9 for DICE-RL. On a shared
diffusion base for robomimic square, CAST reaches 90\% success with
approximately 38\% fewer environment steps than DICE-RL. A five-task
LIBERO ablation shows why the reference matters: removing all restoration yields zero
success even with clipping. Initial OpenArm evaluations show gains of
40 and 35 percentage points over the placement and stacking bases.
Alternative constraints and transfer studies reveal task-dependent limits.
Together, the results support a persistent reference to pretrained
behavior as a practical way to preserve competence while allowing adaptation.
