\method{} constrains the actions the actor is asked to learn. We adopt
DICE-RL's frozen-base residual architecture and off-policy training
loop~\cite{dicerl2026}, replacing its actor objective with regression onto
corrected action targets. Fig.~\ref{fig:method-update} follows one update:
sample current actions, displace them using the critic, apply restoration
and clipping, and fit the residual to the resulting targets.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:cast} places these steps in the online learning loop.
All action-space operations use normalized coordinates, with dimension
$D=Hd$.

\subsection{Sample and Improve Actions}
\label{sec:reward-field}

\textbf{Step 1: sample (Fig.~\ref{fig:method-update}, upper left).}
For each replay state $s$, draw $K$ noise samples. Pair each base action
$a_{0,i}=\pi_0(s,z_i)$ with its current residual:
\begin{equation}
  \bar r_i=\operatorname{sg}[r_\theta(s,z_i)],
  \qquad a_i=\pi_0(s,z_i)+\bar r_i,
  \label{eq:candidates}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}$ denotes stop-gradient. The blue base and gray
current-actor distributions in the figure remain fixed during target
construction; each correction refers to the base action with the same noise.

\textbf{Step 2: improve (upper right).}
Let $\bar Q_\phi(s,z,a)$ be the conservative ensemble aggregate, omitting
$z$ when the critic does not use it. Normalize its action gradient by the
detached scale
$q_{\mathrm{scale}}=\max(\epsilon,\mathbb{E}_{\mathrm{batch}}[|\bar Q_\phi|])$,
computed over the current candidates. The orange displacement is
\begin{equation}
  d_i^Q=\eta_Q\,C_{\delta_Q}\!\left(
    \frac{\nabla_a\bar Q_\phi(s,z_i,a_i)}{q_{\mathrm{scale}}}
  \right),
  \label{eq:reward-step}
\end{equation}
where $C_\delta(v)=v\min(1,\delta/\|v\|_2)$, with $C_\delta(0)=0$.
The solid orange arrow is the accepted displacement; the dashed continuation
shows the critic's larger request. Clipping preserves small gradients and
rescales large ones before applying $\eta_Q$. We differentiate the critic
with respect to actions only, holding its parameters fixed for this step.

\subsection{Constrain the Target}
\label{sec:anchor}

\textbf{Step 3: restore and clip (lower right).}
A small step can still accumulate into a large departure. We therefore
measure each candidate's distance from its frozen reference in rms units,
$m_i=\|\bar r_i\|_2/\sqrt{D}$, and add the restoring displacement
\begin{equation}
  d_i^R=-\eta_0\bar r_i
        -\eta_{\mathrm{anc}}
        \left(1-\frac{\rho}{\max(m_i,\epsilon)}\right)_{+}\bar r_i,
  \label{eq:restoring-step}
\end{equation}
where $(x)_+=\max(x,0)$. The weak first term always pulls toward the base.
The second permits a dead zone of radius $\rho$ and opposes only the excess
departure beyond it. The rms definition keeps the radius comparable across
chunk dimensions. Unlike a reference to the preceding iterate, the frozen
base does not move as training progresses.

The green arrow in Fig.~\ref{fig:method-update} adds $d_i^R$ head-to-tail
to the orange proposal. This visual translation represents vector addition:
the restoring field is evaluated at $\bar r_i$, not at the displaced action.
We then cap the \emph{combined} displacement and detach the target:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde r_i=\operatorname{sg}\!\left[
    \bar r_i+C_{\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}}(d_i^Q+d_i^R)
  \right].
  \label{eq:field-target}
\end{equation}
The green diamond lies within $\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}$ of the current action,
as indicated by the dotted circle. Thus
$\|\tilde r_i-\bar r_i\|_2\leq\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}$.
The two controls serve different purposes: restoration opposes accumulated
departure from the base, while clipping limits the correction requested in
one update. Restoration is soft; it does not project the target into the
base-centered dead zone.

\subsection{Fit the Residual}
\label{sec:field-update}

\textbf{Step 4: regress (lower left).}
The corrected green cloud from step 3 becomes a set of fixed targets.
We fit the residual network with mean-squared error:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal L_{\mathrm{actor}}
    =\mathbb{E}_{s}\!\left[
      \frac{1}{KD}\sum_{i=1}^{K}
      \|r_\theta(s,z_i)-\tilde r_i\|_2^2
    \right].
  \label{eq:actor-regression}
\end{equation}
Only this loss updates the actor. The base remains frozen, and the detached
targets prevent the actor loss from differentiating through the critic.
Freshly constructed targets leave executed replay transitions intact.
The method needs no policy likelihood; deployment uses
$\pi_0(s,z)+r_\theta(s,z)$.

The constraint bounds the \emph{requested target displacement}, not the
realized change after fitting a shared neural network. Similarly, the
dead-zone field has a loss-space counterpart proportional to
$(\|r\|_2-\sqrt{D}\rho)_+^2$, but this correspondence does not make the
finite training updates equivalent. Section~\ref{sec:experiments} compares
the restoring field with a hinge penalty and a hard target projection.
