We evaluate the actor update and the constraints that govern its departure
from the pretrained policy. Controlled comparisons match the frozen base,
critic-training procedure, replay settings, and interaction budget.

\subsection{Training Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

We alternate online collection with critic and actor updates, using the
DICE-RL training framework~\cite{dicerl2026}. The critic retains ensemble
temporal-difference learning, $n$-step returns, and demonstration replay.
Online replay is initialized with base-policy rollouts, and minibatches mix
online experience with demonstrations. Exploration uses the baseline's
warm-up and value-guided candidate-selection procedure. Actor updates and
target-critic updates follow their configured schedules. These settings
are held fixed within each controlled actor comparison.

\textbf{Actor-update settings.}
The LIBERO recipe uses $K=16$, $\eta_0=0.05$,
$\eta_{\mathrm{anc}}=1$, $\rho=0.05$ rms, $\delta_Q=1$, and
$\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}=0.15$. We compare shared reward steps
$\eta_Q\in\{1,2\}$ across tasks. The robomimic recipe uses
$\eta_Q=0.5$ and $\rho=0$. Thus the dead-zone setting is benchmark-specific,
while the target-construction rule is the same.

\subsection{Constraint and Reward-Field Controls}
\label{sec:exp-controls}

\textbf{Constraint factorial.}
The constraint factorial independently switches step clipping and
restoration on or off. Disabling clipping removes both $C_{\delta_Q}$ and
$C_{\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}}$; disabling restoration sets both $\eta_0$ and
$\eta_{\mathrm{anc}}$ to zero. Setting $\rho=0$ instead makes the restoring
terms always active, and increasing $\rho$ alone leaves the weak pull intact.

\textbf{Alternative restoring mechanisms.}
We compare full \method{} with a dead-zone hinge in the backpropagated
actor's loss and a hard projection of the action target. The LIBERO hinge
control uses radius $0.05$ rms and BC weight $100$. The LIBERO projection control
caps the residual target at $0.05$ rms, with both soft restoring terms and
step clips disabled. These comparisons test the implementation of the
constraint as well as its shape; related geometry need not produce equally
reliable finite training updates.

\textbf{Reward-field ablations.}
For reward-field ablations, we replace the action gradient with particle
transport~\cite{deng2026drifting} toward either the critic's top-$k$
candidates or candidates weighted by a softmax of standardized
$Q$-values. Clipping, restoration, and regression remain the same,
isolating the contribution of the critic's gradient direction.
