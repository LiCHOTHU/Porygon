\subsection{Fine-Tuning and Steering Generative Policies}

Reward feedback can enter a generative policy through its training objective
or its sampling procedure. DPPO optimizes the diffusion denoising
process~\cite{ren2024dppo}; ReinFlow introduces stochastic flow transitions
with tractable likelihoods~\cite{reinflow2025}; and Flow Policy Optimization
uses a flow-matching loss surrogate~\cite{mcallister2025flowmatchingpolicygradients}.
Diffusion-QL combines diffusion behavior cloning with value
maximization~\cite{wang2023diffusionql}, while Flow Q-Learning trains a
one-step actor with critic guidance and distillation
regularization~\cite{park2025fql}.

A frozen policy can also be improved by changing how its samples are
obtained or selected. DSRL learns the input noise
distribution~\cite{wagenmaker2025dsrl}, and V-GPS ranks sampled actions
using an offline-trained value function~\cite{nakamoto2025vgps}. IBRL
uses imitation-policy proposals for exploration and value
bootstrapping~\cite{hu2024ibrl}. CAST operates on the generator's output:
it learns residual action corrections while retaining the frozen base's
sampling procedure.

\subsection{Critic-Guided Action and Particle Updates}

Critic guidance can shape action targets or the objective used to train
a generative policy. DIPO improves stored actions by critic-gradient
ascent~\cite{dipo2024}, and PA-RL combines candidate ranking and local
action optimization with supervised fitting~\cite{mark2024parl}.
Q-Score Matching (QSM) instead relates the policy score to the critic's
action gradient~\cite{psenka2024qsm}.
AWR, MPO, and IDQL also use weighted fitting or action
selection~\cite{peng2019awr,abdolmaleki2018mpo,hansenestruch2023idql}.

Particle updates connect CAST to Soft Q-Learning's amortized Stein
sampler~\cite{haarnoja2017sql} and drifting models' displaced-sample
regression~\cite{deng2026drifting}. Drifting Field Policy combines top-$k$
critic-selected targets with drift losses that anchor behavior~\cite{dfp2026}.
CAST uses fresh residual targets to study how improvement and preservation
should be combined. Its main variant pairs each candidate with its
same-noise base output, adds radial restoration to critic guidance, and
clips the requested correction. Value-only variants examine alternatives
to the local action gradient.

\subsection{Residual Refinement and Behavioral Constraints}

Residual RL augments existing controllers with learned
corrections~\cite{johannink2019residual}. ResFiT extends this approach to
frozen action-chunked BC policies under sparse rewards~\cite{ankile2025resfit}.
Policy Decorator bounds residual outputs and progressively introduces
exploration~\cite{yuan2025policydecorator}; DAWN addresses critic warm-up
and scale mismatch~\cite{dawn2026}. DICE-RL combines residual value
maximization, selective behavior regularization, and value-guided action
selection~\cite{dicerl2026}.

Behavioral constraints also limit exploitation of critic error in offline
RL. BEAR restricts actions toward dataset support~\cite{kumar2019bear},
while TD3+BC adds a cloning penalty to the actor
objective~\cite{fujimoto2021td3bc}. Fisher Decorator constrains residual
transport using Fisher geometry estimated from flow-policy
scores~\cite{cheng2026fisherdecorator}. CAST applies radial restoration and
per-candidate displacement caps to explicit action targets, using only
samples from the base. Its restoring terms depend on departure from the base, independently of the critic's
preference for the current action. Within DICE-RL, we test whether clipping
alone can replace this reference and compare the resulting update with
loss-space penalties and hard target projection.
