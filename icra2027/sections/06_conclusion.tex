CAST combines critic-guided action improvement with a restoring reference
to the pretrained policy and a cap on the target displacement. Its local
gradient interpretation connects it to regularized actor learning, while
its explicit targets make the action-space correction available before
fitting the actor. The robomimic and LIBERO comparisons show useful gains
at controlled budgets, and the constraint factorial demonstrates that
small steps alone can still destroy pretrained behavior. Alternative
constraints can be stronger on individual tasks; the evidence favors
consistent refinement rather than universal superiority of one update.
On OpenArm, all three refinements improve observed success over the base
on both Jigglypuff placement and stacking. CAST achieves 19/20 and 14/20
successes, respectively, versus base counts of 11/20 and 7/20. Its margins
over DICE-RL are one and two trials, while BC outperforms DICE-RL on
stacking. These results add physical evidence for refinement, with larger
evaluations needed to resolve differences between update rules.

The failure cases in Sec.~\ref{sec:exp-dmc} show that anchoring can be
counterproductive with a weak reference or a shared multitask critic.
DMC further shows that neither sparse reward nor a dead-zone interior
alone determines which update works. A target bound is not a theorem about the
learned policy's outputs, and the radius is not derived from critic
reliability. Adapting that radius to justified local trust is a natural
next step. Broader physical evaluation is needed to assess how these
findings generalize across tasks and initial conditions.
