\subsection{Reward-step sweep}
\label{app:eta-sweep}

\method{}'s reward step $\eta_Q$ is the one hyperparameter we varied. We report
the whole sweep here because the per-task optima disagree, and because the main
tables deliberately do \emph{not} use them: every \method{} number in
\cref{tab:libero-single} runs a single shared $\eta_Q$ across all eight tasks,
the same courtesy the baselines get by being reported at their published
recipes. Tuning per task would raise our numbers --- $0.880$ instead of
$0.802$ on the red-mug task --- and would not be a like-for-like comparison.

The flow-matching rows also correct an earlier reading of ours. At
$\eta_Q{=}1$ \method{} sits \emph{below} the base on the front-caddy task
($0.667$ against $0.683$) and gains only $+0.003$ on the eight-task mean; at
$\eta_Q{=}2$ the same task gains $+0.100$. The flow-matching column was
therefore under-tuned rather than resistant to the method, and we had
previously attributed its flat result to limited headroom --- an explanation
the per-task numbers do not support, since gains are the same
($+0.003$ mean) for tasks whose base is below $0.72$ and above it.

\subsection{Measured diagnostics behind the mechanism claims}
\label{app:diagnostics}

Three claims in the main text rest on direct measurements rather than on
outcomes. We give the numbers here so they can be checked.

\paragraph{(i) How weak the shared critic's action signal is.}
\cref{tab:libero} reports that no update rule moves the shared-critic base
under a single noise draw. The measurement below shows why a
\emph{gradient-following} update struggles there: the shared critic barely
changes its value when the action moves, so the direction a method would climb
is dominated by noise. We flag one limit of this diagnostic. It also reports a
narrow spread across the $K$ candidates, which would predict that
\emph{selection} fails too --- and \cref{tab:multitask-bon} shows it does not:
best-of-$16$ selection recovers $5.4$ points for DICE-RL and $2.3$ for
\method{}. A spread of $0.7\%$ is evidently still enough to rank candidates
usefully, so this table explains the weakness of the gradient channel, not an
absence of signal overall.

\paragraph{(ii) Why QSM collapses on the first update.}
QSM's actor loss retrains the denoiser to emit $\eta\,\nabla_a Q$ in place of
the noise it was pretrained to predict, with no behaviour-cloning term. Whether
that is survivable depends on whether the two are on the same scale. They are
not: measured on the pretrained square base with a freshly initialised critic,
$\|\nabla_a Q\| = 0.0127$ against $\|\epsilon\| = 5.212$. At QSM's published
coefficient the target is $2.4\%$ of what the denoiser currently outputs, so
the pretrained map is overwritten rather than adjusted --- consistent with the
observed $0.284 \rightarrow 0.000$ within a single evaluation interval. No
coefficient resolves this: reaching parity needs $\eta \approx 400$, at which
point the denoiser is copying a random critic.
(\texttt{scripts/diag\_qsm\_scale.py} reproduces this in seconds.)

\subsection{The multitask setting}
\label{app:multitask}

\cref{tab:libero-single} fine-tunes each task with its own residual head and
critic, routed by task ID at deployment --- the architecture published LIBERO
RL fine-tuners use. The alternative is one policy and one critic trained across
all eight tasks jointly. Under the single-noise-draw evaluation used throughout
this paper, no arm moves its base by more than seed noise
(\cref{tab:libero}), and \cref{tab:critic-diag} supplies a mechanism: $16$
candidate actions scored within $0.7\%$ of one another, and a $0.05$ action
step moving $Q$ by under one part in forty.

That reading turns out to be an artefact of the evaluation, and we correct it
here. A single noise draw reproduces the base whenever the learned residual is
near zero, so it cannot distinguish ``the update rule did nothing'' from ``the
update rule learned something the evaluation never samples.'' Re-scoring the
\emph{same} checkpoints under the best-of-$N$ critic selection that DICE-RL's
own LIBERO evaluation uses ($N{=}16$, \texttt{max\_q\_min}) separates the two,
and the picture changes (\cref{tab:multitask-bon}): DICE-RL gains $5.4$ points
over the shared-critic base and \method{} gains $2.3$, so the shared critic
does carry usable signal --- it is only unusable when a single draw has to find
it. We report this plainly because it cuts against us: in the multitask setting
\method{} improves on its base but \emph{trails} DICE-RL by $3.0$ points,
reversing the per-task ordering of \cref{tab:libero-single}. Our displacement
bound is what keeps the residual close to the base, and when the critic is this
weakly action-sensitive that conservatism costs more than it protects. We take
this as the honest boundary of the method rather than a tuning deficit.
