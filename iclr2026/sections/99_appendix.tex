\subsection{Reward-step sweep}
\label{app:eta-sweep}

\method{}'s reward step $\eta_Q$ is the one hyperparameter we varied. We report
the whole sweep here because the per-task optima disagree, and because the main
tables deliberately do \emph{not} use them: every \method{} number in
\cref{tab:libero-single} runs a single shared $\eta_Q$ across all eight tasks,
the same courtesy the baselines get by being reported at their published
recipes. Tuning per task would raise our numbers --- $0.880$ instead of
$0.802$ on the red-mug task --- and would not be a like-for-like comparison.

\caption{Reward-step sweep, powered evaluation (last-3 checkpoints, single
training seed). The optimum is task-dependent and non-monotonic: $\eta_Q{=}4$
is best on the red-mug task, $\eta_Q{=}1$ on ketchup, and $\eta_Q{=}8$ is worst
on both. $\eta_Q{=}2$ is best on neither individually but has the best mean
across the eight tasks of \cref{tab:libero-single}, which is why it is the
setting we ship. On the flow-matching base the ordering differs again ---

The flow-matching rows also correct an earlier reading of ours. At
$\eta_Q{=}1$ \method{} sits \emph{below} the base on the front-caddy task
($0.667$ against $0.683$) and gains only $+0.003$ on the eight-task mean; at
$\eta_Q{=}2$ the same task gains $+0.100$. The flow-matching column was
therefore under-tuned rather than resistant to the method, and we had
previously attributed its flat result to limited headroom --- an explanation
the per-task numbers do not support, since gains are the same
($+0.003$ mean) for tasks whose base is below $0.72$ and above it.

\subsection{Measured diagnostics behind the mechanism claims}
\label{app:diagnostics}

Three claims in the main text rest on direct measurements rather than on
outcomes. We give the numbers here so they can be checked.

\paragraph{(i) Why nothing works in the multitask setting.}
\cref{tab:libero} reports that no update rule moves the shared-critic base.
The cause is upstream of every method: the shared critic barely changes its
value when the action moves, so both mechanisms these methods rely on --- a
gradient to climb, and a spread over candidates to select from --- are starved
at once.

\paragraph{(ii) Why QSM collapses on the first update.}
QSM's actor loss retrains the denoiser to emit $\eta\,\nabla_a Q$ in place of
the noise it was pretrained to predict, with no behaviour-cloning term. Whether
that is survivable depends on whether the two are on the same scale. They are
not: measured on the pretrained square base with a freshly initialised critic,
$\|\nabla_a Q\| = 0.0127$ against $\|\epsilon\| = 5.212$. At QSM's published
coefficient the target is $2.4\%$ of what the denoiser currently outputs, so
the pretrained map is overwritten rather than adjusted --- consistent with the
observed $0.284 \rightarrow 0.000$ within a single evaluation interval. No
coefficient resolves this: reaching parity needs $\eta \approx 400$, at which
point the denoiser is copying a random critic.
(\texttt{scripts/diag\_qsm\_scale.py} reproduces this in seconds.)
