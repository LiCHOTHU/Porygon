Our experiments answer three questions. \textbf{Q1:} Does the field-target
\emph{form} beat backpropagation given the \emph{same} reward signal and
budget? \textbf{Q2:} Can a one-step drifting base fine-tuned with \method{}
overtake the stronger flow-matching pipeline? \textbf{Q3:} When should the
reward field use the critic's gradient versus only its values --- does the
regime picture of \cref{sec:toy} hold on real benchmarks?

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:exp-setup}

\textbf{Benchmarks.} (i) \emph{robomimic} can and square (state-based;
300-episode evaluations, average of the last three checkpoints; 3 seeds),
run inside the official DICE-RL harness with only the actor update
replaced --- critic, replay, demonstrations, and evaluation protocol are
byte-identical to the baseline. (ii) The \emph{eight hardest tasks of
LIBERO-90} (multitask drifting base; 100 RL iterations; final numbers use
powered evaluation: 100 rollouts $\times$ 3 evaluation seeds).
\todo{appendix table: env details, horizons, demo counts, budgets.}

\textbf{Compared methods.} All arms share the drifting base, residual
parameterization, critic, and data; only the actor update differs:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A} (control): the DICE-RL residual actor --- backpropagated
  $-Q$ with a filtered BC penalty \citep{dicerl2026} --- on our drifting base;
  \item \textbf{B} (\method{}): \cref{eq:update} with the gradient reward
  field $V_Q^{\mathrm{grad}}$;
  \item \textbf{C} / \textbf{T}: the same update with the zeroth-order
  top-$k$ / tilted reward fields (the resolution dial);
  \item \textbf{FM+DICE}: the unmodified state-of-the-art pipeline on the
  flow-matching base \citep{dicerl2026}.
\end{itemize}
Hyperparameters are \emph{family-shared} across B/C/T ($\eta_Q{=}0.5$,
$\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}{=}0.15$, $\eta_{\mathrm{anc}}{=}1.0$, $\tau{=}1$,
$k{=}4$; dead zone $\rho{=}0.05$ on LIBERO) so that comparisons isolate
the update rule rather than tuning effort.

\Cref{tab:robomimic} gives the per-seed results. On can, \method{} reaches
$0.97$--$0.99$ from a base at $0.877$, above both the backpropagated control
on the same base ($0.950$) and the flow-matching pipeline ($0.957$) --- a
weaker base plus a safer update overtakes a stronger base plus the standard
update (\textbf{Q2}). On square, the harder task, \method{} attains
$0.92$--$0.94$ on every seed. \todo{complete Q1/Q2 square comparison
sentences when the matched-budget A and FM+DICE square runs finish.}

\emph{Why does the form win?} The trust region converts critic error
from a stability threat into bounded noise: with policy motion capped at
$\delta_{\mathrm{tot}}$ per update, \method{} tolerates a reward step
($\eta_Q{=}0.5$) far above what the backpropagated control can survive,
and simply takes more, safer steps in the directions where the critic is
consistent. The control must instead keep its effective step small enough
for its \emph{worst} critic errors, which is exactly the plateau behavior
we observe \todo{quantify with the stability-window ablation
(\cref{sec:exp-ablation}, iii) and the matched-budget A square endpoint}.

The dial answers \textbf{Q3} in its first regime: with a critic that
generalizes (robomimic critics see dense, single-task data), the value-only
reward fields preserve can ($0.90$--$0.96$) but collapse on square
($\sim\!0.6$ versus \method{}'s $\sim\!0.93$), despite verified-healthy
residuals and longer training. Ranking resolution throws away exactly the
signal that solves the harder task --- the real-benchmark mirror of the
toy's easy regime, where the compass wins.
