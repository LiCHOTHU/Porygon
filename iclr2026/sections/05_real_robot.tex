\subsection{Real-robot fine-tuning on stair-based manipulation tasks}
\label{sec:exp-real-robot}

We extend the study to physical manipulation on an OpenArm platform, asking
whether post-training can improve a demonstration-trained policy when the
feedback available after each attempt is a human success/failure label.
The real-robot study is in progress: the task setup and demonstrations are
available, while the four-way quantitative comparison below remains to be
completed. This study tests refinement of an existing skill rather than
learning a new policy from scratch.

\paragraph{Platform and tasks.}
The robot operates its left seven-joint arm and gripper using RGB images from
a chest-mounted camera and a left-wrist camera, together with left-arm joint
and gripper positions and a fixed task-language prompt. We consider two tasks:
\emph{Jigglypuff into the case}, in which the robot grasps the toy from a stair
tread and releases it into an adjacent open case; and \emph{red-on-purple
stacking}, in which it picks up the red cube and places it on the purple cube.
Both use a hand-made staircase. The stepped support surfaces introduce
different approach heights and nearby edges, making grasping and placement
sensitive to alignment; stacking additionally requires the upper cube to be
positioned accurately before release. \Cref{fig:real-robot-tasks} illustrates
the tasks through successful teleoperated demonstrations from both views.

\paragraph{Pretrained policy and refinement methods.}
For each task, we first train a flow-matching BC policy on teleoperated
demonstrations. The current policy predicts 30-step chunks of absolute
left-arm joint and gripper targets at 15\,Hz, using a 10-step Euler sampler.
The deployed interface executes a prefix of each chunk before replanning.
The comparison uses one fixed base checkpoint per task and four conditions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Base FM:} deploy the demonstration-trained checkpoint without
  online refinement.
  \item \textbf{Success-only BC refinement:} initialize from that same
  checkpoint and continue supervised flow-matching training using only
  successful online rollout trajectories. Failed attempts are excluded from
  these imitation targets.
  \item \textbf{DICE-RL:} fine-tune a residual actor and critic over the same
  frozen base using both successful and failed online rollouts, with the
  standard DICE-RL actor objective~\citep{dicerl2026}.
  \item \textbf{DICE-RL $+$ \method{}:} use the same residual RL setup and both
  rollout outcomes, replacing the actor update with \method{}'s clipped,
  base-restoring action-target regression.
\end{itemize}
Thus all three refinement conditions start from pretrained weights; none
trains a task policy from scratch. Success-only BC tests how much improvement
can be obtained by imitating successful practice, while the two RL conditions
test whether failed experience and the choice of actor update yield further
benefit.

\paragraph{Rollout collection and evaluation protocol.}
The evaluation interface records camera observations, measured robot states,
and executed commands during each attempt. After stopping the robot, an
operator assigns success or failure and chooses whether to retain the
trajectory. The label is the sparse task-outcome feedback; it is not inferred
from the BC loss or a critic score. For the RL comparison, valid failed trials
must be retained alongside successful ones, with discarded or invalid trials
accounted for explicitly to avoid success-selection bias.

The planned comparison matches the starting checkpoint, robot-interaction
budget, reset and object-placement distribution, and evaluation conditions.
DICE-RL and DICE-RL $+$ \method{} additionally share the critic architecture,
critic-training procedure, demonstration-replay settings, exploration, and
update schedule, isolating the actor update. All methods use the same
observation/action interface and execution limits. Replay transitions must
correspond to the commands actually executed, including chunk-prefix execution
and command limiting, rather than unexecuted predictions. Evaluation trials
are held out from all refinement data.

We will count Jigglypuff placement as successful when the toy is released
fully inside the case and remains there, and stacking as successful when the
red cube remains supported by the purple cube after release and gripper
withdrawal. A common post-release observation interval and matched trial
counts will be fixed before scoring the four methods. \Cref{tab:real-robot}
reserves the success-rate comparison; the demonstration sequences establish
the task setup but do not supply any of its quantitative entries.
