\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

We consider an episodic decision process with states $s$, action chunks
$a \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times d}$ ($H$ control steps of a $d$-dimensional
action, treated as a single vector of dimension $S = Hd$), and sparse
task-success reward. A \emph{one-step generative policy} maps a state
and a noise sample to an action in a single network evaluation,
\begin{equation}
  a = \pi_0(s, z), \qquad z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I),
\end{equation}
and is pretrained on demonstrations with a drifting-model objective
\citep{deng2026drifting}: at each step, a population of policy samples
is transported by a kernel velocity field toward the data distribution,
and the network is regressed onto the transported targets. We write
$\mathcal{V}(x \mid p^{+}, p^{-})$ for this transport field acting on
query particles $x$, attracted to particles of $p^{+}$ and repelled from
particles of $p^{-}$ (\cref{sec:method} recalls its exact form).

Following residual fine-tuning practice \citep{dawn2026,dicerl2026}, we
freeze the base and learn a residual head on top of it,
\begin{equation}
  a = \pi_0(s,z) + r_\theta(s,z),
  \label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
together with an ensemble critic $Q_\phi(s, z, a)$ trained by temporal
difference with a conservative (CQL-style) penalty
\citep{kumar2020cql}. The critic training is standard and identical for
every method compared in this paper; \emph{the object of study is
solely the actor update} --- how the policy consumes the critic.

\subsection{Two structural failure modes}
\label{sec:failure-modes}

\paragraph{No likelihoods.} Policy-gradient fine-tuners
(PPO/GRPO-style, e.g.\ SimpleVLA-RL \citep{simplevlarl}) require
$\log \pi(a \mid s)$. A one-step generator defines its action
distribution implicitly through the noise map \cref{eq:residual}; its
likelihood is intractable, and unlike multi-step diffusion policies
there is no chain-MDP reformulation to fall back on
\citep{ren2024dppo,park2025fql,reinflow2025}. The ratio-based toolbox
is structurally unavailable.

\paragraph{Critic exploitation through backpropagation.} The remaining
standard update --- backpropagate $-Q$ through the actor, as in DDPG
lineage methods and in DICE-RL's residual actor \citep{dicerl2026} ---
couples the \emph{size} of the policy update to the \emph{magnitude} of
the critic's action-gradient: $\nabla_\theta \mathbb{E}[-Q] =
-\nabla_a Q \cdot \partial a / \partial \theta$. A learned critic is
only trained to \emph{rank} actions near the data manifold; nothing
constrains its gradient field off-manifold, where it can be both steep
and wrong \citep{fujimoto2018td3,fujimoto2019bcq,savo2025}. A
steep-but-wrong gradient therefore marches the policy off the data
manifold at maximum speed --- the better the critic looks to the
optimizer, the worse the true policy becomes.

\subsection{A two-regime toy experiment}
\label{sec:toy}

We isolate the second failure mode in a bandit with a bimodal expert
distribution, a one-step base pretrained with the drift operator, and a
critic fit \emph{only} on on-manifold data. We compare the standard
backpropagation update without and with a BC anchor
($-Q$ and $-Q + \lambda \|r\|^2$, the DICE-RL residual actor) against
the field-target update of \cref{sec:method}, in two regimes
(\cref{fig:toy}; 3 seeds).

\textbf{Easy regime} (2-D actions, dense critic data): the critic
generalizes, its gradient field is trustworthy, and backpropagation
wins --- true reward $0.793$ ($-Q$) and $0.770$ ($-Q$ + anchor) versus
$0.530$ for a value-only field update and $0.496$ for the base. When
the critic is a good compass, following its gradient is the right thing
to do.

\textbf{Hard regime} (16-D actions, 60 critic training points ---
mimicking high-dimensional chunked control with scarce coverage): the
same backpropagation update collapses to $0.000$ true reward while its
internal estimate soars ($Q$ rises by $2.9\times 10^{4}$; 100\% of
actions off-manifold). Critically, \emph{the BC anchor does not save
it}: $-Q + \lambda\|r\|^2$ also reaches $0.000$ (99\% off-manifold).
Penalty terms compete with an unbounded gradient and lose. The
field-target update, whose displacement is clipped in action space and
anchored by a restoring field, stays entirely on-manifold and holds the
base's performance ($0.614$ vs.\ base $0.647$) while preserving action
dispersion (best-of-16 sampling reaches $0.767$).

Two conclusions set up the method. First, the failure is
\emph{regime-dependent}: gradient-following is optimal exactly when the
critic's gradients generalize, and catastrophic when the critic can only
rank. Any fix must retain the first-order signal where it is good.
Second, what protects the field update in the hard regime is not the
choice of reward signal but the \emph{form} of the update: displacement
bounded pointwise in action space, plus a restoring anchor field ---
properties a backpropagated loss cannot express, since gradient
clipping in parameter space bounds neither. This motivates a method
that keeps the safe update form and plugs in the strongest reward field
the critic supports.
