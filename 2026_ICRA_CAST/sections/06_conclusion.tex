CAST uses a pretrained policy as a persistent reference during critic-guided
refinement. It constructs action targets by combining critic proposals
with radial restoration and displacement caps, then fits a residual actor
to those targets. Matched manipulation studies show substantial success
gains and earlier improvement; physical placement and stacking provide
initial evidence for refinement from recorded practice.

The component study gives the clearest evidence for restoration: both
updates without it lose all success, including the clipped variant.
Preservation must nevertheless leave room for adaptation. Hard projection
and value-only guidance lead on individual tasks, while transport and
shared-critic multitask comparisons favor DICE-RL. DMC further shows that
sparse rewards alone do not explain the manipulation failures. These
results make the strength and form of the reference a design choice
whose value depends on the task.

The analysis characterizes target corrections and candidate distributions;
it does not guarantee successful behavior after fitting. Adapting
restoration to measured local critic reliability, together with larger
physical evaluations, is a next step. The goal remains to retain useful
parts of a skill while learning the corrections needed to complete it.
