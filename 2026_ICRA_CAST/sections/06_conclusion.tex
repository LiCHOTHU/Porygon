We presented CAST, a likelihood-free method for refining pretrained
generative robot policies through residual learning. CAST combines
critic guidance with soft restoration toward the frozen base and clips
each proposed correction to construct fixed action targets. The residual
then learns these targets by regression. Experiments with state-based and
visual manipulation policies show improvements in task success and fewer
interactions needed to reach a target success rate. Dense- and
sparse-reward control tasks provide further evidence of useful refinement,
while offline OpenArm experiments demonstrate gains in physical placement
and stacking.

The central design lesson is to maintain a reference to useful behavior
while allowing the changes needed for improvement. In the five-task
LIBERO component study, restoration preserves task success where clipping
alone fails. The constraint and guidance comparisons also show that
stronger preservation is not uniformly better: its value depends on the
task and the proposed correction. This balance is relevant to robot skills
that already reach and grasp successfully but still need to improve
placement or release. Adapting restoration to local critic reliability
and expanding physical evaluations are the next steps toward retaining
these useful behaviors while learning more reliable task completion.
