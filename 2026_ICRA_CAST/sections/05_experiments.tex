We evaluate whether CAST improves pretrained skills and which update
choices make refinement useful through three hypotheses:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{H1 (useful refinement):} CAST improves task performance or
reaches a target success rate with fewer environment steps than standard
residual refinement.
\item \textbf{H2 (a persistent reference):} Restoration toward the frozen
base helps preserve task performance beyond limiting individual corrections.
\item \textbf{H3 (preservation and adaptation):} The strength and form of
preservation affect refinement; stronger constraints need not yield
better performance.
\end{itemize}

\label{sec:exp-setup}
Our evaluation spans state-based robomimic manipulation~\cite{mandlekar2021robomimic},
image-based LIBERO-90 manipulation~\cite{liu2023libero}, dense- and
sparse-reward DMC control~\cite{tassa2018dmc}, and physical OpenArm
placement and stacking. Robomimic and LIBERO use sparse task-success
rewards; DMC broadens the reward settings and task family. The physical
study evaluates offline refinement from recorded experience followed by
autonomous robot trials. Together, these settings test different observation
modalities, reward structures, and deployment conditions. Simulation and
physical comparisons test H1, the component comparison tests H2, and
alternative constraints and restoration-radius sweeps test H3. DMC extends
the evaluation to dense- and sparse-reward control.

Our primary comparison uses DICE-RL's frozen-base residual framework and
critic-training procedure. DICE-RL optimizes critic value with selectively
applied BC regularization; CAST learns constrained action targets. Matched
simulation comparisons share the pretrained checkpoint, residual
architecture, replay recipe, and budget, although collected experience and
learned critics can diverge. We therefore compare the complete actor
updates. Success rates are percentages and gains are percentage points
(pp); additional protocol details are in the experimental supplement.

\subsection{Policy Improvement in Simulation}
\label{sec:exp-libero}
\input{sections/tables/05_libero}

\textbf{Improvement across visual tasks.}
To test H1 on visual manipulation, we first train each multitask base on
all 90 LIBERO tasks, then select eight challenging tasks for refinement.
Each selected task receives its own residual policy and critic over the
shared frozen base, with task-ID routing at deployment. Both base classes
use the same eight-task subset. Table~\ref{tab:libero-main} reports two fixed values
of the actor's critic-guidance scale, $\eta_Q$, each shared across all tasks.
On the drifting base, CAST$_2$ raises mean success from 66.1\% to 75.5\%
(+9.4 pp), compared with 67.0\% for DICE-RL (+0.9 pp).
Both CAST settings improve over every task's base. The benefit includes
avoiding degradation: on red mug, DICE-RL falls below the base, whereas
both CAST settings improve it substantially. DICE-RL still leads on
Study-3 caddy.

The flow-matching base makes the dependence on the starting policy and
update setting clear. Its mean success is 75.3\%; CAST$_1$ reaches 75.6\%
and CAST$_2$ reaches 77.7\%, compared with 75.0\% for DICE-RL. The gain
is smaller, and neither CAST setting improves every task. For example,
all refinements fall below the strong juice-placement base, while doubling
$\eta_Q$ helps front-caddy placement but hurts right-caddy placement.
The higher mean success over the eight selected tasks supports H1 across
both base classes, while the task-level results show how the guidance
scale affects individual skills.

\textbf{Refinement across generator classes.}
\label{sec:exp-robomimic}
\input{sections/tables/05_base_models}
Table~\ref{tab:base-models} complements the visual study with state-based
robomimic refinement on drifting, diffusion, and flow-matching bases. On drifting-base
square, CAST improves a 38.2\% base to 91.2--93.0\%, exceeding DICE-RL
on each of three training seeds at 20K updates. Substantial improvement
is therefore compatible with a restoring reference to an imperfect base.
CAST also improves can from a stronger starting policy, and its value-only
variant improves flow-matching square from 57.0\% to 93.4\%.
These gains support H1 across generator classes. The diffusion comparison
below tests improvement speed separately; additional protocol details and
external baseline comparisons are in the supplement.

\input{sections/05_efficiency_figure}
\textbf{Environment steps to useful performance.}
A higher final success rate and earlier improvement are distinct benefits.
On the shared diffusion base for square, CAST first reaches 90\% success
at a mean of 149.3K environment steps, compared with 240K for DICE-RL
(Fig.~\ref{fig:diffusion-efficiency}). This is approximately 38\% fewer
steps. At the common 496K endpoint, success is similar: 95.9\% for CAST
and 96.5\% for DICE-RL. Reaching the success threshold with fewer
environment steps supports H1's efficiency claim.

\subsection{The Role of Restoration and Clipping}
\label{sec:exp-controls}
\input{sections/05_components_figure}

Is limiting each correction sufficient, or must refinement also respond
to accumulated departure from the base? The five-task LIBERO component
comparison in Fig.~\ref{fig:component-comparison} evaluates no controls,
clipping only, radial restoration only, and full CAST. The first two
remove both restoring terms. Radial restoration alone also sets
$\eta_0=0$, whereas full CAST enables the weak pull, radial restoration,
and both action-space caps. This is a component comparison: the last
contrast changes both clipping and the weak restoring term.

Both configurations without restoration reach zero reported success on
all five tasks, even when each requested correction is capped.
Restoration variants retain nonzero success throughout. This supports H2
in the tested setting: a cap on individual corrections does not replace
a persistent reference. The distinction follows the update's design:
clipping limits the current request, while restoration responds to the
departure already accumulated from the frozen base.

Retaining some success does not ensure improvement. Radial restoration
alone averages 65.7\%, below the base's 67.3\%, and improves only two
tasks. Full CAST reaches 70.3\% and improves every task over its base,
although radial restoration alone remains better on red mug and juice.
Together, these results support the full update as a useful balance
between retaining pretrained behavior and improving task performance.

\subsection{Choosing the Constraint and Critic Guidance}
\label{sec:exp-design}
\input{sections/tables/05_update_design}

\textbf{Does harder preservation help?}
To test H3, Table~\ref{tab:update-design}(a) compares a penalty in the
actor loss, a hard constraint on the target, and CAST's soft restoration.
The \emph{loss-space hinge} uses $(m-\rho)_+^2$ as DICE-RL's residual
penalty, where $m=\|r_\theta(s,z)\|_2/\sqrt{D}$ is the RMS departure
from the base, while retaining its BC filtering. \emph{Hard target
projection} rescales the residual target to satisfy
$\|\tilde r_i\|_2\leq\sqrt{D}\rho$, a ball centered on the base action.
CAST instead restores toward the base while capping the target's
\emph{step from the current action} in \eqref{eq:field-target}.
The LIBERO projection recipe replaces restoration and both caps;
auxiliary settings for these update recipes are given in the supplement.

CAST reaches 97.0\% on square, compared with 96.0\% for projection,
and leads on ketchup at 68.0\%. Projection remains best on mug
(93.0\% versus 78.0\% for CAST), but falls below the ketchup base.
The hinge improves square from 38.2\% to 48.0\%, but remains well below
projection and CAST. This task-dependent ordering supports H3:
stronger preservation does not uniformly improve refinement. Restricting
departure can retain useful behavior while also limiting useful changes;
the observed reversals are consistent with this tradeoff.

\textbf{Can value-only guidance replace action gradients?}
Table~\ref{tab:update-design}(b) tests whether refinement can use the
critic's scores without differentiating them with respect to actions.
Gradient guidance proposes a local correction; the two value-only
variants instead use kernel-weighted attraction and repulsion.
\emph{Top-$k$} attracts candidates toward the $k$ highest-valued current
samples. \emph{Value-tilted} guidance weights all sampled attractors
proportionally to $\exp(\hat q_i/\tau)$, where $\hat q_i$ is the critic
value standardized within the state's candidate set and $\tau>0$ is a
temperature.
Both repel from uniformly weighted current samples. Their weak
restoration uses independent base samples in place of the paired pull
$-\eta_0\bar r_i$; radial restoration and displacement caps are retained
with the settings reported in the supplement.

The gradient recipe performs best on square and ketchup. Both value-only
recipes improve over the square base but fall below the ketchup base.
On mug, value-tilted transport exceeds the gradient recipe. These results
show that value-only guidance can improve a pretrained policy, while the
relative benefit of action gradients depends on the task. This connects
H1's refinement gains to the choice of critic guidance.

\textbf{Allowing room before stronger restoration.}
The radius $\rho$ determines when the stronger restoring term activates.
At $\rho=0$, it acts on every nonzero residual. In exploratory
single-training-seed sweeps on mug and ketchup, positive radii from
0.02 to 0.20 improve success over this setting. For example, $\rho=0.05$
raises mug success from 54.9\% to 78.1\% and ketchup success from 60.1\%
to 68.4\%. This provides exploratory support for H3: allowing local
adaptation before stronger restoration can improve refinement. The
supplement reports every radius and result. As $\rho\to\infty$, the weak pull remains, so that
setting still differs from removing restoration. Together, these studies
show why both the proposed correction and the way it is moderated matter.

\subsection{Refinement under Dense and Sparse Rewards}
\label{sec:exp-dmc}
\input{sections/tables/05_dmc_main}

Table~\ref{tab:dmc-main} reports DMC refinement of one-step bases
distilled from SAC experience under dense and sparse rewards. With dense
rewards, CAST improves swing-up from 429 to 715 return and balance from
378 to 911. With sparse rewards, it improves balance from 90 to 1000
and ball-in-cup catch from 86 to 925. These gains extend the evidence for
H1 beyond manipulation to both reward settings. Unconstrained refinement
also improves the two sparse tasks, showing that the observed role of
restoration varies across control settings. The table caption and
supplement provide evaluation and training-budget details.

In summary, Tables~\ref{tab:libero-main}--\ref{tab:base-models} and
Figs.~\ref{fig:diffusion-efficiency}--\ref{fig:real-robot} support H1
through higher success or earlier improvement. Fig.~\ref{fig:component-comparison}
supports H2 by showing that clipping alone does not preserve performance.
Table~\ref{tab:update-design}(a) and the radius sweep support H3: stronger
preservation does not uniformly help. The appropriate balance depends on
the refinement setting.
